# Designing Candy

Candy is a personal tool for spending time with ideas and getting work done. It should feel compact, alive, and unmistakably its own. The pleasure of using it matters throughout an ordinary working day, from opening it to navigating, waiting, reading, and returning to a task.

This philosophy guides design judgment. The [terminal guide](packages/coding-agent/docs/usage.md) describes current interactions; [AGENTS.md](AGENTS.md) defines implementation and validation practices.

## A playful kind of neon

Yellow, purple, and teal establish Candy's visual character. Neon light, retro digital textures, and cyber aesthetics give it energy; warmth, playful shapes, and small expressive details make that energy approachable. Its cuteness should feel intentional and well crafted.

Compose color through relationships: bright accents need quieter surroundings, a highlight needs somewhere to stand out, and a dense view needs a readable rhythm. A restrained surface can make a vivid detail more expressive. Carry the same character through light and dark appearances with appropriate contrast.

Candy has an indie sensibility: recognizable personal taste, affection for small details, and room for surprise. A tip, an unexpected phrase, a tiny visual gesture, or the rhythm of a transition can express that taste. These touches should feel naturally at home in the product. Their form can change as Candy develops.

## Beauty is part of the work

Beauty and motion are requirements of a finished design. Give them the same attention as speed, clarity, and useful behavior. A feature is ready when its ordinary use feels considered: spacing, color, emphasis, transitions, feedback, and the way it fits into the surrounding experience.

When these qualities compete, work on the composition and interaction until they support one another. Use hierarchy to make a crowded view readable, tune animation to make a fast action feel immediate, and put detail at the point where it helps a decision. The goal is a balanced experience that keeps its visual pleasure.

Polish includes the states people repeatedly encounter: waiting, empty results, cancellation, errors, and returning from another view. These deserve the same care as a successful first impression.

## Invent the interaction

Be bold about how a terminal tool can work. Explore spatial controls, direct manipulation, animated transitions, and unfamiliar combinations when they make an action more satisfying or natural. Existing conventions provide useful knowledge; new interaction can establish its own logic through use.

An invention earns its place through a complete experience. People should be able to discover it, understand what responds to their input, make a choice, change their mind, and continue working. Clear feedback and coherent behavior help a distinctive interaction become familiar.

Let related actions share an understandable rhythm. Keep a user's draft, place, and sense of direction as they move through the interface. A design can ask people to learn something new and reward that learning with fluency.

## Compactness through hierarchy

Keep useful choices close to the work and give each view a clear emphasis. Spend space on information that helps someone recognize an object, understand its state, or choose an action. Reveal further detail when it becomes useful.

Small terminals still deserve a refined composition and expressive motion. Preserve identifying information and the quality of the layout, then provide a deliberate way to inspect the rest. Wider terminals can offer richer context while retaining the same hierarchy.

Compactness should make the next action easier to find. Judge density by how comfortably someone can read, navigate, and decide over time.

## Motion gives the interface life

Motion contributes personality as well as meaning. Timing, easing, a little anticipation, and a satisfying finish can make everyday actions feel tactile and playful. Use movement to connect states, express activity, and make a change feel understood.

Balance that expression with the user's attention. Give reading a stable foundation and give interaction an immediate response. Continuous activity needs a rhythm that remains comfortable over a long session; brief actions can have their own small moments of delight.

Input should remain responsive throughout a transition, and fast navigation should follow the user's latest intention. Activity should correspond to real work, and completion should feel distinct. People who reduce motion should still receive a carefully composed experience with clear states and feedback.

Evaluate Candy by using it over time. Its character should survive repeated actions, long waits, dense conversations, narrow windows, and interruptions. The aim is a tool whose usefulness and personal charm make it a pleasure to keep open.
